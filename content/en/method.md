# Our Method

Stand Up for Justice Madagascar acts as a citizen collective for monitoring, documentation, and public engagement. Our role is to receive information, organize it carefully, and bring serious situations to the attention of competent authorities or institutions.

{{< alert >}}
We do not replace courts, investigators, or victims’ lawyers. Published information is used to document, alert, and request that facts be examined by competent authorities.
{{< /alert >}}

## 1. Receiving a Report

A report may come from a victim, a relative, a witness, an engaged citizen, or a public source. We ask only for useful information: facts, date, location, people concerned, available documents, and the level of risk for witnesses.

{{< button href="/contact/" label="Report a case" />}}

## 2. Verifying Carefully

Before any publication or transmission, we seek to cross-check information: date consistency, location, documents, testimonies, public sources, and available material evidence. When a fact is not confirmed, it must be presented as such.

## 3. Protecting Exposed People

The safety of victims, families, and witnesses comes before public communication. We may anonymize names, remove sensitive details, or delay publication if this reduces risk.

## 4. Documenting Follow-Up

When the case allows it, we keep a record of actions taken: letters sent, institutions contacted, transmission dates, replies received, and changes in the case status.

## 5. Engaging Institutions

Depending on each case, the collective may send reports or correspondence to public authorities, national institutions, international bodies, or human rights organizations.

## Our Principles

- Citizen independence
- Caution in accusations
- Respect for victims’ dignity
- Witness protection
- Transparency about the limits of our information
- Refusal of partisan instrumentalization
